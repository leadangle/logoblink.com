<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">A lot of praise could be said for the talented <strong>graphic designers</strong> from all over the world and their work. This post will open a word about the ones who made their way from<strong> Japan</strong>. The Japanese graphic designers’ work embodies <strong>rich culture</strong>, traditions, colors, shapes and concepts, which are a way far from the western world’s tendencies. After the war the country of the “rising sun” turned out with a big potential in the field of culture and industry. Even without the historical knowledge one could easily remark how creative, abstract and unique the <strong>Japanese art</strong> is, especially when it comes to the logotypes of the country’s municipalities. Their <strong>design</strong> fully possesses the nerve to be labeled as unique and avant-gardish, because it<strong> successfully distinct</strong> from the trivial ideas of trees, fountains and sun shines, which are so popular in the Western world.</span></span>

<img class="alignnone size-full wp-image-4001" alt="Japanese graphic design" src="/img/2014/05/Japanese-grpahic-design-lobolink.jpg" width="831" height="727" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">Every<strong> Japanese municipal</strong> has its own<strong> symbol</strong> with different meaning. A quick eye over the logos of random cities will leave the impression that every place has tried to beat the other with the most original concept, catching the <strong>city’s identity</strong>. Though each place’s logo is strongly unique, there is a common element that the most observant ones will notice– most of the designs stick to the <strong>simple style</strong> of the Japanese flag.</span></span>

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><b>Fuji, Shizuoka</b></span></span>

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-3982" alt="Japanese graphic design" src="/img/2014/05/FujiShizuoka.jpg" width="300" height="249" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">What a beautiful <strong>green</strong> image, </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">right? I truly hope you do not</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> take it as a reference to the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerald_City"><strong>Emerald city</strong></a>, because it’s not. </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">Neither it’s</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> about <strong>Oz</strong>, n</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">or</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> <strong>Kansas</strong> at all. Actually it’s about the beautiful mountain<strong> Fuji</strong>. And there is a nice city named Fuji as well, most of which holds a pretty good view to the gorgeous mountain. The logo represents the <strong>mountain</strong> and refer to 1966 when three municipalities merged to form the current city.</span></span>

**<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><b>Hamada, Shimane</b></span></span>**

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-3985" alt="Japanese graphic design" src="/img/2014/05/HamadaShimane-1.jpg" width="300" height="264" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">Hamada is a city on the<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Japan"><strong> Sea of Japan</strong></a> and posses beautiful white sand beaches. Respectively the <strong>logo represent</strong>s sea waves and a beach, as there is a kanji symbol in the middle. The style resemblance the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Japan">flag of Japan</a> and embodies the<strong> unique nature</strong> of the city’s location.</span></span>

**<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">Ibusuki, Kagoshima</span></span>**

<img alt="Japanese graphic design" src="/img/2014/05/IbusukiKagoshima.jpg" width="300" height="245" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">The current city<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibusuki,_Kagoshima"> <strong>Ibusuku</strong> </a>was merged from three towns in 2006. And this is what the three white lines in the logo</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> above</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> stand for. The stylized letters ‘I’ and ‘U’ are reference to</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> all of the </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">vowell</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">s</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> in the city’s name. The <strong>butterfly</strong> is one of Ibusuku’s <strong>symbols</strong>, which explains the reddish prettiness in the middle.</span></span>

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><b>Imabari, Ehime</b></span></span>

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-3988" alt="Japanese graphic design" src="/img/2014/05/ImabariEhime.jpg" width="300" height="259" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">Imabari’s logo shows<strong> three elements</strong></span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">,</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> none of which, </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">regardless the shape,</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> is Pepsi. <strong>Land, water and a bridge</strong> – they represent the <strong>Inland Sea</strong>, the bridge over<a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurushima-Kaiky%C5%8D_Bridge"> <strong>Kurushima Strait</strong></a> and the green coast. </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">Features </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">that could not be seen in a lot of p</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">laces gathered together in such a unique way</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">. Whether the stylized ‘I’ symbolize</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">s</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> the Inland Sea or the city’s name is u</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">n</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">known, but let’s suppose it </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">refer</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> to both.</span></span>

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><b>Itami, Hyogo</b></span></span>

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-3990" alt="Japanese graphic design" src="/img/2014/05/ItamiHyogo.jpg" width="300" height="250" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><strong>The Koya Pond</strong>, one of the most popular stopovers for migratory birds, is located in <strong>Itami city, Hyogo</strong>. T</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">h</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">e meaning of the logo embraces the <strong>uniqueness</strong> of the Pond as the concept shows <strong>blue background</strong> and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji"><strong>stylized kanji</strong> </a>as a water bird.</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> Simple and great. “<strong>Less is more</strong>” in its full potential.</span></span>

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><b>The Japanese Gotham city ;)</b></span></span>

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-3992" alt="Japanese logo design" src="/img/2014/05/Flag_of_Fukuyama_Hiroshima.png" width="300" height="200" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">The symbol of<strong> Fukuyama</strong> city represents a mountain shaped as bat. Considering the <strong>black and yellow</strong> colors t</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">he comic fans probably will be disappointed to learn that the bat does not stand for Batman’s cave. It has more to do with the</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><strong> Fukuyama Castle</strong>,</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> which is </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">set on a </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">location known as <a href="http://jet.wikia.com/wiki/Fukuyama"><strong>The Bat Mountain.</strong></a></span></span>

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><b>Hakusan, Ishikawa</b></span></span>

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-3994" alt="Japanese graphic design" src="/img/2014/05/HakusanIshikawa.jpg" width="300" height="264" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><strong>Sea, river and a mountain</strong> – three in one for the city of <strong>Hakusan’s logo</strong>. Their particular meaning is connected to the Teodori River, the Sea of Japan and the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Haku">Mount Hakusan</a>. <strong>Simple and sticky</strong>.</span></span>

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><b>Noshiro, Akita</b></span></span>

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-3996" alt="Japanese graphic design" src="/img/2014/05/NoshiroAkita.jpg" width="300" height="268" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">The city of <strong>Noshiro</strong> is located in the Akita Prefecture and is bordered by the Sea of Japan to the west. The specific location of the place is visualized in </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">the city</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">‘s logo – the blue element represents the Sea of Japan and the green part is the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirakami-Sanchi">Shirakami Mountains</a>. </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">Quite minimalistic</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">, but</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> very</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> descriptive – a </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">praise that every logo dreams for.</span></span>

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><b>Ogasawara, Tokyo</b></span></span>

<img class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-3998" alt="Japanese graphic design" src="/img/2014/05/OgasawaraTokyo.jpg" width="300" height="262" />

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"><strong>Ogasawara</strong> is a village that governs the Bonin Islands, Volcano Islands and three remote islands. The logo shows a disk and three birds within. The birds stand for peace and unification of the islanders. The logo </span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">does</span></span><span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;"> a good reference to harmony and progress and does it in a truly unique way.</span></span>

<span style="color: #777777;"><span style="font-family: 'Lucida Sans Unicode', sans-serif;">This humble selection of <strong>signs</strong> on the flags of the Japanese municipals is a good index to where this country stands on the<strong> world map of art and design</strong>. Nevertheless more academics are starting to talk about the<strong> Japanese influence</strong> on the western world’s graphic design, which is quite controversial to the majority of westernization theories not just in the graphic design, but in all academic fields as well. So the next time we open a word about Japan, we’ll do a praise to some <strong>big names</strong> from this field as well. Stay tuned to learn more about the richness of this culture.</span></span>
